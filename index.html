About

I trained as a mechanical engineer. That meant learning to think in systems — constraints, forces, feedback, failure modes. Before a thing is built, you have to model it. And a good model isn't a picture of reality; it's a compression of the parts that matter.

I came to AI because it sits at the same intersection: reasoning, behavior, and real-world consequence. But the tools for understanding AI behavior are underdeveloped. We have benchmarks that measure performance on narrow tasks, not the cognitive structures underneath them.

That gap is where I want to work. I'm especially interested in evaluation design — building assessments that surface how systems (human or AI) actually reason — and in human-AI interaction, where the quality of collaboration depends on how well each side models the other.

My engineering background gave me rigor. What I'm building now is the vocabulary to apply that rigor to behavior, cognition, and the systems we're making.
